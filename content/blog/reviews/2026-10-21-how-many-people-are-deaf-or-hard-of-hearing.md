## Required fixes (if FAIL)

1. Table "The numbers at a glance", last-but-one row. Change `| People with hearing loss by 2050 | nearly 2.5 billion, one in four | WHO [2] |` to `| People with hearing loss by 2050 | nearly 2.5 billion, one in four | WHO [2], [6] |`. Reason: [2] has "nearly 2.5 billion" but not "one in four". [6] says: "Nearly 2.5 billion people worldwide ─ or 1 in 4 people ─ will be living with some degree of hearing loss by 2050". Write it as "[2], [6]", not "[2][6]": the citation parser skips a marker that is followed directly by "[".

2. "Will the number..." section, second paragraph. Change "It also warns that over 1 billion young adults are at risk of permanent, avoidable hearing loss from unsafe listening, such as loud music through headphones [2]." to "It also warns that over 1 billion young adults are at risk of permanent, avoidable hearing loss from unsafe listening practices [2]." Reason: [2] says "Over 1 billion young adults are at risk of permanent, avoidable hearing loss due to unsafe listening practices." It never mentions music or headphones.

3. "What do deaf, hard of hearing..." section, second paragraph. Change "Many deaf people use a sign language as their first or main language." to "For many deaf people, sign language is an important way to communicate [6]." Reason: no source supports "first or main language". [6] says "the use of sign language and other means of sensory substitution such as speech reading are important options for many deaf people".

4. Date the US survey figure. In the US and UK table, change the first US row's figure "About 15% of adults, 37.5 million people, report some trouble hearing" to "About 15% of adults, 37.5 million people, reported some trouble hearing in the 2012 National Health Interview Survey". In FAQ 3, change "About 15% of American adults, roughly 37.5 million people aged 18 and over, report some trouble hearing, according to the National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders." to "About 15% of American adults, roughly 37.5 million people aged 18 and over, reported some trouble hearing in the 2012 National Health Interview Survey, according to the National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders (NIDCD)." Reason: PLAYBOOK, data template: "Say the year each number refers to". NIDCD [4] gives this source for the line: "Blackwell, DL; Lucas, JW; Clarke, TC (2014): Summary health statistics for U.S. adults: National Health Interview Survey, 2012".

5. "Who has hearing loss?" section, first sentence. Change "Age is the biggest factor." to "Hearing loss becomes more common with age [2]." Reason: the claim is uncited and no source ranks age as "the biggest factor". [2] says "The prevalence of hearing loss increases with age". The new sentence also names its subject.
   In the same spirit, in "Where do people with hearing loss live?", change "These are also the places with the fewest specialists." to "Low-income countries also have the fewest specialists." Reason: [6]'s figures ("Among low-income countries, about 78% have fewer than one ear, nose and throat (ENT) specialist per million population; 93% have fewer than one audiologist per million") are about low-income countries only. "These places" also points back to the Western Pacific region, which includes Japan and Australia.

6. "Will the number..." section, first paragraph. Change "The number grows because there will be many more older people [1]." to "The number grows because the world's population is growing and getting older [1]." In FAQ 6, change "The number of people with hearing loss is rising, but mainly because populations are ageing." to "The number of people with hearing loss is rising, mainly because the world's population is growing and getting older." Reason: [1] (full text) says "increases in prevalent cases are driven by population growth and ageing".

7. Explain 403 million vs 430 million, which sit in adjacent table rows and look like a contradiction. Change the table row label "Moderate or worse hearing loss, even with hearing aids" to "Moderate or worse hearing loss, after accounting for hearing aid use". After the paragraph that starts "The two headline numbers measure different things.", add: "The WHO's 430 million comes from the same Global Burden of Disease estimate: 430.4 million people had moderate or worse hearing loss in the better ear in 2019, and 403.3 million still did after accounting for the hearing aids some of them use [1]." Reason: [1] says "403.3 million ... had hearing loss that was moderate or higher in severity after adjusting for hearing aid use, and 430.4 million ... without adjustment", and "This study accompanies the 2021 WHO World Hearing Report, which describes the burden of hearing loss using the GBD 2019 results". PLAYBOOK: "If sources disagree, show both and explain why".
