Five apps do live captions for conversations in the room, and each one is the best choice for someone. Google Live Transcribe is free on Android [4]. Apple's Live Captions is already on the iPhone [1]. Ava is built for group conversations [8], Otter for meetings [12], and Captio AI, a live captioning app for deaf and hard of hearing people, for iPhone and Android with translation and saved recordings [13]. This roundup uses each company's own pages, opened in November 2026, and says where each app is the weaker choice, Captio AI included.

## How were these live caption apps compared?

The apps were compared on what each maker says on its own website, help centre or store listing in November 2026. The test was in-person speech: a deaf or hard of hearing person puts a phone on the table and reads what the people around them say. Apps that caption phone calls, video calls or online meetings were not compared on those features. Prices are US prices as of November 2026. Where a page does not say something, the table says "not stated" instead of guessing.

## Which live caption app is best for everyday conversations?

For everyday conversations, such as coffee with a friend, the built-in apps are the easiest start and Captio AI or Ava are the better choice when you want names and a saved record. Apple says Live Captions can capture and caption live, in-person speech through the microphone in your device [1]. Google describes Live Transcribe as an app that turns speech into text on an Android phone [3]. Ava tells people to keep the phone within 1 to 2 feet of the speaker [8], and the same habit helps with any app. For [everyday conversations](/use-cases/everyday-conversations) on iPhone or Android where you also want to keep the chat, Captio AI saves each recording and Live Captions shows the speakers.

## Which live caption app is best for groups and family dinners?

Ava and Captio AI are the two built for groups, because both separate speakers. Ava's help page says it can tell voices apart, shows each participant in a different colour, and lets guests join by QR code or link with no download, each phone acting as a microphone [8]. Ava also warns that background noise lowers accuracy in public places [8]. Captio AI labels speakers as Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 and lets you rename them. On iPhone, Recognize voices can show the name of someone you saved, such as "Mum" instead of "Speaker 2". For a [group setting](/use-cases/group-settings) like a family dinner, see also [how to see who is talking in live captions](/blog/how-to-see-who-is-talking-in-live-captions).

## Which live caption app is best for lectures and meetings?

For a lecture, free session length decides first. Ava's free plan allows captioned sessions of up to 40 minutes [6], Otter's free plan allows up to 30 minutes per conversation and 3 lifetime file imports [11], and Captio AI's free plan is 100 free credits a day, which is counted differently and is not a session length. Otter's paid Pro plan raises the limit to 90 minutes per meeting, at US$16.99 a month or US$8.33 a month billed annually on Otter's pricing page [11]. The App Store listing shows Otter Pro at US$16.99 monthly or US$99.99 yearly [12], so check which price your store shows. In a [lecture](/use-cases/lectures) or a meeting, Captio AI keeps the recording with its audio in a searchable library, and an AI Summary can turn it into key points or to-dos. Otter's free plan also lists speaker identification and an AI Chat limit of 20 queries a month [11].

## Which live caption app is best for a doctor's appointment?

For a [doctor's appointment](/use-cases/doctor-appointments), pick the app you already know, test it before the visit, and ask the clinic first. Live captions are not a replacement for an interpreter or a human captioner, and a medical team may need to approve recording. Captio AI lets you add names and medical terms to Words and names so they are recognised. Ava's help page says speakers should hold the phone close to their mouths in noisy places [8], which a small consulting room rarely needs. Ava's pricing page labels its paid Community plan "Not an ADA-compliant plan" [6], which matters only if you need to ask a provider for access.

## Which live caption app is best for travelling?

When you travel and the other person speaks another language, translation matters more than speaker labels. Captio AI's [Live Translator](/live-translator) shows live translation next to the original captions in 60+ languages, and a language hint tells it what to expect. Ava's own pages differ on translation: its help page says it is available only with Premium captions, for English with French, English with Spanish, English to Turkish and French to German [7], its pricing page says 20+ languages [6] and its App Store listing says 16 languages [10]. Check the app for your pair. Apple's Live Captions list covers 8 languages in regional variants, with no translation described on the pages cited [2]. Live Transcribe's pages describe captions in the spoken language [3]. A deaf traveller asking at a station counter in Italy would want [translation as well as captions](/blog/how-to-caption-a-conversation-in-two-languages).

## What about Android Live Caption?

Android's Live Caption is for sound playing on the phone, not for a conversation across the table, as far as Google's page says. Google describes it as captions for media such as videos, podcasts, phone calls, video calls and audio messages, processed on the device [5]. The page does not say it captions people speaking in the room through the microphone, so for that use Live Transcribe [3]. It is the reason a phone can show captions on a video without any app.

## Where each live caption app is the better choice

Each app wins somewhere, and it is not always Captio AI.

- **Google Live Transcribe wins on price and offline use on Android.** It lists 0 USD on Google Play [4], over 80 languages and dialects [4], and offline languages on Android 12 or later [3]. Transcripts are kept for up to 3 days only if you ask [3]. The full comparison is in [Captio AI vs Google Live Transcribe](/blog/captio-ai-vs-google-live-transcribe).
- **Apple Live Captions wins on being already there.** It needs no download, and Apple's setup list also names Mac, Apple Watch and Apple Vision Pro [1]. Its language list is shorter, at 8 languages [2].
- **Ava wins on group sessions with guests.** Guests join with a QR code and need no download [8], and the free plan has no limit on group size [10].
- **Otter wins for meetings in a work team.** Its free plan gives 300 monthly transcription minutes and it connects to Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet [11]. Its pricing page lists English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Chinese [11].
- **Captio AI is the better choice for live translation next to captions, and for saved recordings with audio, on iPhone and Android.** Its limits: live captions need internet, the free plan is 100 free credits a day, captions pause when you leave the app on Android, and Recognize voices, Speaker direction and Recognize sounds are iPhone features. It does not do sign language and it is not for phone calls. See also the page comparing [Captio AI and Ava](/compare/captio-ai-vs-ava).

## Which live caption app should you pick?

Start with your phone, then with the situation:

- **Android, free, quick chats:** Google Live Transcribe.
- **iPhone, nothing to install:** Apple Live Captions, in the languages Apple lists [2].
- **A family table with several voices:** Ava or Captio AI.
- **A meeting with a work team:** Otter, or Captio AI if you also want live translation.
- **Travel or a conversation in two languages:** Captio AI.
- **A long lecture you need notes from:** a paid plan, such as Captio AI Plus (US$14.99 a month or US$79.99 a year on the US App Store, as of November 2026), or Otter Pro (up to 90 minutes per meeting [11]). Ava's Community plan stays at 40-minute sessions [6]. Free plans have daily or per-session limits.

Try two apps in a quiet room before the day that matters, with the phone near the speaker.
