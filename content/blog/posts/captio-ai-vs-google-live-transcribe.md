Google Live Transcribe and Captio AI both turn speech in the room into text on your phone, and the right one mostly depends on your phone. Live Transcribe is free and Android only [2]. Captio AI, a live captioning app for deaf and hard of hearing people, runs on iPhone and Android and adds live translation, saved recordings and summaries. This comparison uses Google's own pages, opened in November 2026, and says where Google's app is the better choice.

## Captio AI vs Google Live Transcribe at a glance

Live Transcribe is free, already installed on Pixel and select other Android phones [1] and covers over 80 languages and dialects, while Captio AI covers iPhone as well and adds translation, a library and summaries. The table puts both on the same rows, using what each company says on its own pages.

| | Google Live Transcribe | Captio AI |
|---|---|---|
| Phones | Android, needs Android 12 or later on Google Play [2]. Already installed on Pixel and select other Android phones [1] | iPhone and Android |
| Languages | Over 80 languages and dialects [2]; a secondary language can be set [1] | 60+ languages for captions and translation |

## Which one works on iPhone?

Captio AI works on iPhone, and Live Transcribe does not. Google describes Live Transcribe as working "using just your Android phone" [2], and a search of Apple's US App Store for "live transcribe" in November 2026 returned an app of that name from Mighty Fine Apps LLC, not Google [5]. So an iPhone user looking for Google's app will not find it. Captio AI is on the App Store [6] and on Google Play (released October 2026), which helps in a family where a deaf parent uses an iPhone and a hearing child uses Android.

## Which one works without internet?

Live Transcribe works without internet in downloaded languages, and Captio AI does not caption live without a connection. Google says to stay connected to the internet "unless you're using one of the offline languages" [1]. The cloud approach was a deliberate choice: Google Research wrote in 2019 that relying on cloud speech recognition gives greater accuracy [4]. Captio AI needs a connection for live captions, but without one it still records, and the recording is transcribed when the phone reconnects. For a [doctor's appointment](/use-cases/doctor-appointments) in a clinic basement with no signal, an Android phone with a downloaded language is the safer pick.

## Can Live Transcribe show who is talking?

Google's pages do not describe it, while Captio AI separates speakers and can show names. Neither Google's help page nor its Play listing mentions speaker labels [1][2], and Google's 2019 post listed "speaker-separation" among potential future improvements [4]. In a [group setting](/use-cases/group-settings) such as a family dinner, a single stream of text without names is hard to follow. Captio AI labels speakers as Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 and lets you rename them. On iPhone, [Recognize voices](/recognize-voices) can show the name of someone whose voice you saved, like "Mum" instead of "Speaker 2". That feature is iPhone only.

## Which one is better for a lecture or a meeting?

Captio AI is better when you need the transcript afterwards, and Live Transcribe is better when you only need captions in the moment. By default Live Transcribe does not save transcriptions, and you can choose to keep them for 3 days [2]; the help page also lists "Export all stored transcription" in settings [1]. In a [lecture](/use-cases/lectures) or a [meeting](/use-cases/meetings), Captio AI keeps each recording with its audio in a searchable library, and an AI Summary can turn it into key points or to-dos. The free plan includes 3 AI summaries in total. A student who wants notes a week later, or a deaf employee who needs to check a decision, benefits from that.

![Captio AI on iPhone showing a searchable list of saved recordings with titles and dates, as used for lectures and meetings](/LC_mockup_1.png "Captio AI library of saved recordings on iPhone (sample content)")

## Which one is better for travelling or a conversation in two languages?

Captio AI is the one built for translation in conversation. Its [Live Translator](/live-translator) shows live translation next to the original captions, switched on with a Translate toggle.

![Captio AI Live Translator on iPhone with the Translate switch turned on and the language set to English, for travelling or a two-language conversation](/LT_mockup_1.png "Captio AI Live Translator on iPhone (sample content)")

The Live Transcribe pages cited here describe captions in the spoken language and a secondary-language setting [1], and no translation between spoken languages. When you [travel](/use-cases/traveling) and a shop assistant speaks a language you do not, that gap matters.

## Which one is better for hearing a doorbell or smoke alarm?

Live Transcribe, because Sound Notifications always checks for the sounds you pick. Google's help page says the phone "always checks for sounds you want notifications about" once turned on, and lists smoke and fire alarms, sirens, baby sounds, dog barking, knocking, a doorbell, appliance beeping, a landline ringing and running water, with vibration or a camera flash as alerts [3]. It says all the audio the device hears stays on the device [3]. Captio AI's Recognize sounds is different: on iPhone it writes sounds like [doorbell rings] into the captions and makes the phone vibrate for alerts, but only while captions run or a file is transcribed. It is not an always-on alarm.

## Where Google Live Transcribe is the better choice

Live Transcribe is the better choice in six situations, all from Google's own pages:

- **You want free captions with no daily limit.** The Play listing shows a price of 0 USD [2]. Captio AI's free plan is 100 free credits a day.
- **You have an Android phone and often lose signal.** Offline languages work without internet [1].
- **You want one app for captions and sound alerts.** Sound Notifications is part of the same app [2][3].
- **You need more languages.** Google lists over 80 languages and dialects [2], against 60+ for Captio AI, which is counted differently, so check your own language in both.
- **You use sign language as well as speech.** Live Transcribe's help page describes sign-to-text, which turns American Sign Language into English text through the front camera [1]. Captio AI does not do sign language.
- **You want it already on the phone.** Live Transcribe comes installed on Pixel and select other Android phones [1].

## Where Captio AI is the better choice

Captio AI is the better choice when you use an iPhone, need translation, or need the conversation kept. It runs on iPhone and Android, translates live, keeps recordings with their audio, transcribes uploaded audio and video files, and writes summaries. On iPhone it keeps captioning with the screen locked, which matters in a long [conference](/use-cases/conferences) session. On Android, captions pause when you leave the app, and Recognize voices, Speaker direction and Recognize sounds are iPhone features. Live captions need internet. Captio AI does not replace a sign language interpreter or a human captioner, and it is not for phone calls.

## Which one should you use?

Pick by phone first, then by what you need afterwards:

- **Android, a quick chat, no signal:** Live Transcribe.
- **iPhone, any conversation:** Captio AI, since Live Transcribe is not there [5].
- **A lecture or meeting you need notes from:** Captio AI.
- **Everyday conversations such as a family dinner on iPhone:** Captio AI, with saved names for the people you talk to most.
- **Travelling in another language:** Captio AI.
- **Alarms and the doorbell at home on Android:** Live Transcribe with Sound Notifications.

The two are not rivals for everyone. Some people keep Live Transcribe on an Android phone for always-on sound alerts and use Captio AI where they need translation or a record of what was said. For the full row-by-row version with a link to every source, see the [Captio AI vs Live Transcribe compare page](/compare/captio-ai-vs-live-transcribe) [7]. Prices and limits change, so check the store before you decide. Both apps depend on a clear signal from the speaker, so hold the phone, or put it on the table, close to the person talking: Google gives the same tip for Live Transcribe, to hold the device microphone near the person or sound you want to capture [1].
