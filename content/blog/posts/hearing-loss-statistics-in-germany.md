32.5% of adults in Germany, nearly 1 in 3, report difficulties hearing [1]. That is the newest national figure, from the Robert Koch Institute (RKI), Germany's public health institute, in a paper published on 19 August 2026 [1][2]. It comes from a survey, not a hearing test, and Germany has no register that counts everyone with hearing loss. This page puts the German numbers side by side, says what each one measures, and links them to daily life in Germany.

## The numbers at a glance

The German figures differ because each counts something different. Read the "what it counts" column before you quote one.

| Measure | Number | What it counts | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults reporting hearing difficulties | 32.5% | Self-reported, GEDA 2019/2020, 22,708 adults | RKI [1][2] |
| Adults using a hearing aid | 5.4% | Self-reported, same survey | RKI [1][2] |
| Adults highly hard of hearing or deaf | 0.5% | Self-reported, same survey | RKI [1][2] |
| Hearing loss by WHO classification | 16% | Estimate from the HÖRSTAT study, cited in the Gutenberg paper | Ärzteblatt International [3] |

## How many adults in Germany have hearing difficulties?

32.5% of adults in Germany report hearing difficulties: 33.2% of men and 31.7% of women [2]. The figure comes from the 2019/2020 round of the survey "Gesundheit in Deutschland aktuell" (GEDA), which asked 22,708 adults [2]. The authors say they studied subjectively perceived hearing problems, which differ from measured hearing thresholds [2]. They also found hearing loss was more common among people with lower social support, poorer self-rated health and depressive symptoms [1].

## How does hearing loss in Germany change with age?

Hearing difficulties in Germany become the majority experience after 80. The RKI found 23.3% of adults aged 18 to 49 report them, 37.6% of those aged 50 to 79 and 50.5% of those aged 80 and over [2]. Even among adults under 50, almost 1 in 4 report difficulties [2].

## What does this mean at a doctor's appointment in Germany?

At a German [doctor's appointment](/use-cases/doctor-appointments), hearing difficulties are common among older patients: 50.5% of people aged 80 and over and 37.6% of those aged 50 to 79 report them [2]. A patient who cannot follow the doctor may miss instructions. This is general information, not medical advice. For German speakers, the page on [doctor's appointments in German](/use-cases/doctor-appointments/german) explains how captions fit into a consultation.

## How many people in Germany use a hearing aid?

5.4% of adults in Germany use a hearing aid, and the share rose significantly between 2009 and 2019 [1][2]. The RKI trend analysis covers 109,330 participants across five survey waves since 2009 [2]. The RKI authors conclude that national studies suggest hearing aids are underprovided [1]. The authors of the Gutenberg Health Study paper estimate that only about 37% of people with hearing loss in Germany wear one [3]. The two figures answer different questions: 5.4% is a share of all adults, 37% a share of people with hearing loss.

## Why do German figures say 16% and 32.5%?

The German figure is 16% in one study and 32.5% in another because they measure different things. The RKI's 32.5% counts adults who say they have difficulty hearing [2]. The HÖRSTAT study, cited in the Gutenberg paper, estimated 16% with hearing loss by WHO classification, which rests on measured hearing levels [3]. The same paper cites another German estimate of 19% with a hearing level above 40 dB, and 2% of respondents owning a hearing aid [3]. If you see a single German number without its method, ask which one it is.

## How many people in Germany are deaf?

About 80,000 people in Germany are deaf, according to an estimate by the Deutscher Gehörlosen-Bund (the German Deaf Association) and the Sozialverband VdK [6]. It is an estimate, not a count. The RKI survey found 0.5% of adults highly hard of hearing or deaf, a self-reported share that is much larger than 80,000 people [1][6]. The two figures use different definitions, so do not subtract one from the other.

German Sign Language (Deutsche Gebärdensprache, DGS) has been recognised as a language in its own right since 2002 by the Behindertengleichstellungsgesetz [6]. The sources opened for this page do not give a count of DGS users.

## How many severely disabled people in Germany have a hearing impairment?

Destatis, the Federal Statistical Office, reports about 7.8 million severely disabled people in Germany at the end of 2025, 9.4% of the population [4]. 4% of them had hearing impairments, speech disorders or balance disorders [4]. At the end of 2023 the figure was about 7.9 million, 9.3% of the population, and the same 4% share [4][5]. Destatis says the two years are hard to compare because Hesse and Saxony cleaned up their administrative data, lowering their combined count by about 195,000 [4].

The Federal Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs gave 307,480 people with deafness or hearing loss for 2023, about 0.4% of the population [6]. That counts only people officially recognised as severely disabled through hearing, so the real number is higher [6].

## Do deaf people in Germany have a right to a sign language interpreter in daily life?

In one 2025 Berlin case, yes: the Sozialgericht Berlin ruled on 17 October 2025 that a deaf woman is entitled to sign language interpreting for everyday matters, not only special occasions [7]. The court based the claim on section 78 of Book IX of the Social Code (SGB IX), which covers taking part in community and cultural life, and set a reasonable scope of 8 hours a month [7]. The district social welfare office must pay [7]. The judgment was not final and could be appealed [7]. This is legal information, not legal advice; a Teilhabeberatung or a lawyer can look at your own case.
