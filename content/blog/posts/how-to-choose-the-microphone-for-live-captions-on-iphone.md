To choose the microphone for live captions on iPhone, connect the microphone you want first, then answer the app's question about which one captions should use. Captio AI, a live captioning app for deaf and hard of hearing people, asks that question when AirPods or another microphone is connected, and it remembers your answer [5]. A phone you cannot read from where you sit is no use, so the real choice is often between sitting nearer and using a microphone that is closer to the talker. The advice below is our suggestion unless a source is cited.

## Why does the microphone change what the captions say?

The microphone decides what sound the captions can work with, so a microphone far from the talker gives you worse captions, whichever app you use. A 2021 study in Frontiers in Neuroscience tested 23 adults and 14 children with hearing loss in China. Adults needed the speech to be 5.55 dB louder than the noise at 1.5 m with directional hearing aid microphones, and 13.11 dB louder at 6 m. With a wireless remote microphone, they needed it only to be about 5.5 dB quieter than the speech at 1.5 m, and 7.53 dB quieter at 6 m [4].

The authors report that the remote microphone improved the signal-to-noise ratio by 11 to 19.5 dB for adults and 10 to 18.9 dB for children, and that test distance had no effect on speech-in-noise recognition when it was used [4]. This study compared hearing aid microphones with a wireless microphone near the talker. It did not test phones or AirPods, so it supports one point only: sound picked up near the talker is clearer than sound picked up across a room.

## Which microphone should live captions use in a lecture?

In a lecture, the best microphone is the one nearest the lecturer that you can still read, and that often means sitting nearer. A phone on a front desk would be close to the speaker but too far for you to read. AirPods in your ears hear what reaches you, which from a back row is the weak signal the study above describes [4]. Options, as suggestions: sit in the front rows, ask whether the room has a microphone you can connect to, or use a microphone worn by the lecturer if the lecturer agrees. Captio AI only chooses among microphones connected to your iPhone [5]. See [live captions in a lecture](/use-cases/lectures) and [Live Captions](/live-captions).

## Which microphone suits a meeting or a conference?

For a [meeting](/use-cases/meetings), the phone flat in the middle of the table is our suggestion, because it is within reach of your eyes and nearer to every speaker than your ears are. In a [conference](/use-cases/conferences), a clip-on or room microphone is nearest to the talker, and can be chosen once connected [5]. If you cannot read a phone in the middle of the table, keep it in front of you and sit where the speakers are close.

## Which microphone suits a restaurant or a group dinner?

At a restaurant or a [group dinner](/use-cases/group-settings), the phone on the table between you is our suggestion over AirPods, since the people you want to read are across from you and the phone is where you look. The study shows distance costs clarity for hearing aid microphones [4]. For who said what at a busy table, see [how to see who is talking in live captions](/blog/how-to-see-who-is-talking-in-live-captions).

## How do AirPods choose which side is the microphone?

On iPhone, Apple's default is that either AirPod can act as the microphone, and if you use only one, that one does [2]. You can change it: open Settings, tap your AirPods' name near the top, choose Microphone (or Audio & Routing first, if you do not see it) and pick Automatically Switch AirPods, Always Left AirPod or Always Right AirPod [1]. Apple says the AirPod you choose "becomes the microphone, even if you remove it from your ear or put it in the case" [1].

This Apple setting decides which AirPod listens. Captio AI's question decides which connected microphone captions use, such as AirPods or another microphone [5].

## What does Captio AI do about the microphone?

When AirPods or another microphone is connected, Captio AI asks which one captions should use and remembers the answer [5]. That is all the release notes for version 1.1.6, live on the App Store on 5 November 2026, say about it, and it is an iPhone feature. The notes do not say what the question looks like or whether it appears on Android, so this article does not either.

## What is the difference between Live Listen and captions?

Apple's Live Listen sends sound, and captions show words. Apple says Live Listen makes your iPhone "act like a microphone that sends sound to your AirPods or Beats", and tells you to "Place your iPhone or iPad in front of the person that you want to hear" [6]. It needs iOS or iPadOS 14.3 or later and connected AirPods or Beats [6]. Apple's accessibility page adds that Live Listen sends audio from the iPhone's microphone to compatible AirPods, Beats headphones or Made for iPhone hearing devices [3]. If you need to read the words, not only hear them better, captions fill that gap, and Apple also offers Live Captions on iPhone for in-person speech [3].

## What does choosing a microphone cost?

Captio AI is free to download, and the free plan gives 100 free credits a day, as of October 2026. Captio AI Plus removes the daily limit. The 1.1.6 release notes list choosing your microphone without any plan limit [5].
